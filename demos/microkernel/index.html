Reading the picture

  1. What a microkernel is

    A fixed-size C block (32 × 32 in the real kernel, 8 × 8 here) accumulated across all of K. Inside the loop there is no layout branch, no shape check — just loads, FMOPAs, and prefetch. Everything else was settled before the loop started.

  2. Transpose is absorbed by pack

    Four transpose states collapse into two pack routes: row-strided reads through a ZA transpose (gather), or contiguous reads with a straight copy (stride). The panels come out byte-identical, so the loop never learns which state you chose.

  3. Software pipelining

    Four load slots carry the next k group while the FMOPAs run; B pages are pulled into L2 with pldl2keep and C is streamed with pldl1strm. A leftover K tail runs its own small ldr + fmopa loop, and C leaves through st1w slices plus a non-temporal stnt1w flush.

A and B hold small integers 0–2; C = A × B.
